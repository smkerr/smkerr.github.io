From September 2024 to December 2025, I supported WHO's response to the mpox outbreak. I maintained and developed the [Global Trends dashboard](https://worldhealthorg.shinyapps.io/mpx_global/) in R Shiny, with pipelines in WHO's xMart data platform that automated the upload and validation of data from WHO regional and country offices. I reworked the dashboard to focus on the current outbreak, adding clade-specific trends, laboratory testing and positivity data for the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), and clade I importations. I also worked with the WHO DRC Country Office, the WHO Regional Office for Africa (AFRO) and the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) to agree on common case definitions and aggregation methods, so that the dashboard's figures could be compared across sources.

I produced nowcasts and projections of the reproduction number (Rt), growth rate and cases for the DRC at national, province and health-zone level, with clade-specific estimates on request, as well as for Burundi, Sierra Leone and other affected countries. I prepared analyses and slides for WHO decision-makers, including five Emergency Committee meetings, deep dives for the WHO Health Emergencies Programme (WHE), weekly updates for the Incident Management Support Team and briefings for WHE leadership. I also automated the figures in WHO's regular External Situation Reports and checked each draft against the data before publication. I am a co-author of a [preprint on the multifaceted mpox outbreaks in the DRC](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8195766/v1).

I also built and maintained the DRC Mpox Resource Prioritization dashboard to guide the vaccination response, with weekly data for health zones and the health areas within them, in English and French. WHO staff supporting the vaccination response, the DRC Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (COUSP) and field epidemiologists used it.
